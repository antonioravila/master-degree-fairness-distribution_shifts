% Temporal fairness metrics, as in Almuzaini et al. (2022) -- add your \cite key for [1] where marked.
Following the original ABCInML evaluation protocol, three temporal fairness metrics are computed over the
sequence of Demographic Parity differences of a run, $DP_1, \dots, DP_T$, one value per batch. Each run
yields a single value per metric, and for all three lower values are better:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Maximum Bias}: the worst fairness level reached over time, i.e.\ the peak of the bias,
    \begin{equation}
      \mathrm{MB} = \max_{t} \lvert DP_t \rvert .
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Temporal Stability}: the average change in bias between consecutive batches, measuring how much
    fairness oscillates over time,
    \begin{equation}
      \mathrm{TS} = \frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=2}^{T} \lvert DP_t - DP_{t-1} \rvert .
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Maximum Bias Difference}: the largest jump in bias between two consecutive batches, capturing the
    most abrupt change,
    \begin{equation}
      \mathrm{MBD} = \max_{t \geq 2} \lvert DP_t - DP_{t-1} \rvert .
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Maximum Bias captures how severe the bias becomes, while Temporal Stability and Maximum Bias Difference capture how
unstable it is over time.
